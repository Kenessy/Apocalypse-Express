## Scene B4 — A-Tech Station Offboarder Turret

> [!summary] **SCENE B4 — A-Tech Station Offboarder Turret**  
> TYPE: Main Route, Set Piece, Puzzle Hazard  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: High but survivable  
> PURPOSE: First main-route Station obstacle  
> TEACHING: Multi-path station problem, A-Tech horror, use of prep loot  
>  
> **When this runs:** After **B2 — Chapel of the Second Dawn** and optional **B3 — Greenhouse Supply Run**, when the party reaches the **A-Tech / aSync station** to secure the Apocalypse Express.  
>  
> **Locked design answer:** This is **D + E** from the 2026-05-03 design pass: a **multi-path set-piece** mechanically, with **horror-trap / automated safety-system** tone.

---

### Read This First — Canon Lock

The Offboarder is **not just a combat enemy** and **not just a password puzzle**.

It is an old A-Tech station-defense / platform-safety system still executing its last valid order:

```text
UNAUTHORIZED BOARDERS / OFFBOARDERS: REMOVE FROM PLATFORM.
```

The system is broken only in context, not in logic. It still believes it is protecting the station by removing anything that does not resolve as authorized train/station personnel.

**Core lock:**

- The first A-Tech Station obstacle is the **Offboarder auto-turret / automated station defense** set-piece.
- The best form is **multi-path**: fight, sneak, spoof, disable, or use greenhouse prep.
- The tone is **horror safety-system**: frozen corpses, interrupted boarding, ancient orders still being obeyed.
- PAVNN can help because he reads as A-Tech / SYN maintenance-adjacent, but he is **not full admin** and does not auto-solve it.
- The B3 Greenhouse route can provide practical bypass materials.
- Do **not** make the solution a playable A-Tech robot activation-phrase puzzle.

---

### Station Context — Why It Is Here

> [!abstract] **A-Tech / aSync station setup**  
> This station had A-Tech infrastructure and aSync legacy work on-site. A few pact-bound / Lucifer-contacted aSync personnel had prepared the AI Core for transport before the Halt, but they did **not** finish connecting it to the Apocalypse Express. The core is in a transport/dock state deeper in the station sequence.

This means B4 is the **front-door problem**:

1. Reach the station.
2. Survive / bypass the Offboarder system.
3. Gain access to the locomotive and AI Core dock layer.
4. Then solve the connection / departure puzzles.

The AI Core should be felt as a nearby prize or pressure, but the turret itself remains the first obstacle.

### First View

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Platform approach**  
> The station canopy is still standing, held up by black steel ribs and corporate glass. A-Tech warning lights blink through the frozen dust. Bodies hang where the Halt caught them: a worker halfway over a turnstile, a passenger crouched behind a luggage cart, one hand raised toward a sensor that will never read them.  
>  
> Then something under the platform roof clicks. Smooth. Professional. Awake.  
>  
> A small turret unfolds from the wall and tracks the nearest living thing.

> [!abstract] **Visual anchors**  
> - Frozen boarding massacre: passengers, staff, luggage, ID lanyards, half-open doors.
> - A-Tech signage still bright enough to look official.
> - A platform turret with clean corporate styling, not junkyard scrap.
> - Warning loop: “Offboarder removal active” / “platform integrity protocol” / “unauthorized transit subject.”

### What The Turret Wants

The turret is not angry. It is enforcing categories.

It divides targets into rough buckets:

| Read | Meaning | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized staff / maintenance | Station-safe | ignored or challenged |
| Recognized train asset | transit-safe | warning only |
| Unknown living body | unauthorized boarder/offboarder | target |
| Moving corpse / Halt anomaly | contamination risk | target hard |
| PAVNN | partial A-Tech/SYN maintenance-adjacent read | hesitation / weaker lock |

Use this to keep the set-piece fair: players can infer what the system values by observing what it ignores, hesitates at, or targets first.

### Solution Paths

#### Path A — Combat Hazard

The party can fight it directly.

- Good if they want speed or panic.
- Costs resources, injuries, ammo, tools, or noise.
- Should be possible, but not the cleanest answer.
- Failure should wound / pin / split the party, not hard-kill them.

Suggested stakes:

- Cover breaks.
- Someone is pinned behind luggage.
- A shot cracks a frozen glass panel.
- The turret calls deeper station systems awake.

#### Path B — Puzzle Turret

The clean technical route.

Key elements:

- Line of sight.
- Blind spot under / beside the platform signage.
- Access panel behind cover.
- Exposed power cable or sensor trunk.
- Reset sequence on a cracked A-Tech terminal.

Suggested gate shape:

```text
Observe pattern → reach blind spot → open panel → cut/splice/reset → cross platform.
```

Use visible logic. The player should feel clever, not like they guessed a hidden password.

#### Path C — Authority Spoof

This is allowed, but only as partial spoofing.

Options:

- PAVNN presents as maintenance-adjacent A-Tech/SYN hardware.
- A Construct PC may trigger a weaker “asset” read.
- A recovered employee badge/body tag may buy a few seconds.
- Lucifer pact pressure may produce a supernatural glitch, but not a clean admin login.

Rule of thumb:

```text
Spoofing creates an opening. It does not delete the encounter.
```

#### Path D — Greenhouse Prep Payoff

If the party completed **B3 Greenhouse Supply Run**, let practical loot matter.

Examples:

- Plastic sheeting / reflective glass: blind the turret sensor.
- Hand tools: open access panel without brute force.
- Wire / battery: bridge a reset contact.
- Food crate / water container: decoy weight or rolling cover.
- Greenhouse radio battery: power a dead terminal long enough for one command.

This makes B3 feel like real prep, not filler.

#### Path E — Horror Trap

This is the tone layer, not a separate solution.

Use the horror to explain the rules:

- Corpses show previous failed paths.
- Shot patterns reveal line of sight.
- A dead staff member with a badge shows that authority alone is not enough.
- A worker frozen at the access panel shows the panel is real.
- Luggage and barricades show where cover worked.

The set-piece should feel like reading a crime scene while the weapon is still awake.

---

### Hard Design Constraints

> [!danger] **Do not use A-Tech activation phrase as the puzzle**
>
> A-Tech / aSync activation phrases exist in canon, but they are **not playable puzzle text**. This preserves the campaign rule that players cannot farm dormant A-Tech robots by learning a universal phrase. PAVNN remains the authored exception.

> [!warning] **Do not make PAVNN an instant win**
>
> PAVNN can spoof, carry, assist, analyze, or take risk. He should not solve the entire station defense alone. Best use: he creates a timed window or grants advantage on the technical route.

> [!tip] **Do not make combat mandatory**
>
> This is the first main-route station danger. It should teach the campaign's station logic: observe, infer, use tools, exploit identity systems, and accept consequences.

---

### Suggested Resolution States

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Clean bypass | Party reaches locomotive access with low/no damage; may preserve turret for later station defense |
| Disabled turret | Safe crossing, but station systems register a fault |
| Destroyed turret | Safe crossing, loud/noisy, damages parts that might have been salvage |
| Partial failure | One injury, lost supply, split position, or station alert |
| Retreat | They can fall back, use B3 prep, or choose a rougher combat path later |

No outcome should block the campaign permanently. The worst normal result is arriving at the locomotive under pressure, injured, or with fewer supplies.

---

### Link To The Next Beat

After the Offboarder is handled, the party gets access to the deeper station layer:

- Main locomotive access.
- AI Core transport/dock layer.
- Carriage / connection puzzle.
- Departure prep.
- Lucifer follow-up apparition when they are ready to leave with the train.

The station should now feel like a machine waking up around them.
