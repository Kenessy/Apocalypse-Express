## Scene B5 — A-Tech Station Control Room & Turntable

> [!summary] **SCENE B5 — A-Tech Station Control Room & Turntable**  
> TYPE: Main Route, Puzzle, Station Control  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: Risky but controlled  
> PURPOSE: Secure the Apocalypse Express, assemble departure consist  
> TEACHING: Rail-control puzzle, carriage coupling, station machinery  
>  
> **When this runs:** Immediately after **B4 — A-Tech Station Offboarder Turret**, once the party survives / disables / bypasses the turret and gains access to the station control layer it was guarding.  
>  
> **Locked design answer:** The turret guards the **main control room**. From here the party can control the station rails, turntable, switches, cranes, and carriage positioning enough to assemble the Apocalypse Express and clear a path out of the station.  
>  
> **Story job:**  
> - Pay off the Offboarder as the front-door guard to something important.  
> - Give the party their first real control over the rail infrastructure.  
> - Let them attach / arrange the required starter carriages behind the locomotive.  
> - Make the Apocalypse Express feel like a physical train in a real rail yard, not an abstract menu.  
> - End with the train assembled and ready to depart, triggering Lucifer's next apparition beat.
>  
> **Player job:**  
> - Enter the control room and understand the rail-yard interface.  
> - Use the turntable / switchboard to line up the locomotive and carriage tracks.  
> - Move, clear, or reposition rail obstacles using station machinery.  
> - Decide the starter carriage layout if multiple valid arrangements exist.  
> - Solve enough of the station logic to get the Apocalypse Express out.
>  
> **Tone:**  
> - Industrial control-room puzzle after a horror turret scene.  
> - Dusty corporate rail infrastructure, frozen warning lights, old A-Tech panels, and heavy mechanical movements waking for the first time since the Halt.

---

### Block B5.1 — Turret Room Door Opens

> [!question] **TRIGGER — Entering the control layer**  
> Run this when the Offboarder turret is no longer blocking access. The door / shutter / platform barrier behind it becomes reachable.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The room behind the gun**  
> Past the turret's kill zone, a reinforced door waits under a faded A-Tech rail-control sign. Its lock is scarred by old impacts, but the turret kept it clean of scavengers. When the panel finally accepts a command, the door drags open onto a room full of dead monitors, switch levers, rail diagrams, and one broad circular control display like a brass plate set into the main desk.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Main Control Room**  
> - Elevated view over part of the station yard, platforms, side tracks, and the locomotive bay.  
> - Large control desk with rail diagrams, switch levers, dead screens, and old A-Tech/aSync labels.  
> - A central **turntable / rotating plate control**: this is the key interface for rotating and aligning track segments.  
> - Crane / gantry controls, route-lock panels, platform shutters, and carriage coupling indicators.  
> - Some systems are intact; others are jammed, frozen, or require local confirmation at the yard.

---

### Block B5.2 — What the Control Room Can Do

> [!abstract] **CORE FUNCTION — It controls the yard, not the whole world**  
> The control room is powerful but local. It can manipulate the A-Tech station's rail-yard hardware.

Useful functions:

- **Rotate the turntable / plate** to align the locomotive with different tracks.  
- **Switch rails** to route the locomotive and carriage line toward the departure track.  
- **Move or attach carriages** behind the engine when they are on compatible tracks.  
- **Control a crane / gantry** to move a blocking object, suspended cable, cargo container, or failed station hardware.  
- **Open / release platform locks** that keep the locomotive from departing.  
- **Run limited system hacks** to wake dead panels, override stuck switches, or bypass old lockouts.

> [!warning] **Constraint — No global station admin**  
> This is not a universal A-Tech control console. It cannot solve every later A-Tech problem, recruit random robots, or bypass all future station locks. It controls this departure puzzle.

---

### Block B5.3 — Puzzle Shape

The scene works best as a visible rail-yard manipulation puzzle, not a hidden password puzzle.

```text
Observe the yard state
→ identify the locomotive, carriage tracks, blocked departure line
→ rotate / switch / move pieces
→ couple required carriages
→ clear the departure route
→ release the train
```

> [!tip] **GM — Show the board**  
> Describe the yard like a board state: engine here, carriages there, blocked line there, turntable in the middle. The players should feel like they are manipulating heavy physical pieces.

Possible puzzle elements:

| Element | Problem | Solution style |
|---|---|---|
| Locomotive bay | Engine faces wrong track | Turntable alignment |
| Carriage spur | Starter carriages sit on side tracks | Switch route + couple |
| Blocked departure rail | Cargo / debris / dead carriage blocks exit | Crane / gantry / shunt move |
| Frozen switch | Track refuses to change | SYS hack or local manual lever |
| Platform lock | Station still thinks boarding is unsafe | Control-room release after Offboarder disabled |
| Power dropout | One panel dies mid-puzzle | PAVNN assist, B3 battery, or manual fallback |

---

### Block B5.4 — Starter Carriage Assembly

> [!abstract] **GOAL — Build the departure consist**  
> The party does not need to design the entire train forever. They only need to assemble the initial departure state.

Default expectation:

- **Locomotive / Engine** must be first.  
- Required starter carriages are placed behind it.  
- Optional / damaged / inaccessible cars can be ignored for now.  
- If there is a choice, let players decide which car goes first behind the engine or which utility slot / carriage gets priority.

> [!tip] **GM — Keep it tangible**  
> - Carriages should feel like objects on rails: heavy, delayed, clanking, sometimes misaligned.  
> - Coupling is a physical event: metal jaws closing, brake lines hissing, lights waking along the connection.  
> - If a player asks for a specific placement and it is physically plausible, the control room can usually support it.

---

### Block B5.5 — PAVNN and Skill Use

> [!abstract] **PAVNN's role**  
> PAVNN can interpret A-Tech labels, carry out local yard work, and confirm mechanical statuses. He should make the puzzle clearer, not solve it instantly.

Good PAVNN assists:

- Translates old A-Tech/aSync rail labels.  
- Confirms which carriage tracks are safe to move.  
- Walks to a manual lever while the party uses the control room.  
- Holds a coupling line or checks brake pressure.  
- Gives advantage / soft advantage on one SYS or rail-control step if the party gives him a clear task.

Suggested checks:

- **SYS** to interpret the console, hack a stuck route, or wake dead machinery.  
- **PRY** to force a jammed lever / manual coupling.  
- **AWS** to read the yard layout and spot the simplest route.  
- **RES** if someone has to cross a dangerous yard section or climb onto a carriage.  
- **Riddlecraft / logic equivalent** if the table frames the turntable as a route puzzle.

---

### Block B5.6 — Failure and Pressure

> [!warning] **No hard lock**  
> Failure should create complication, not stop the campaign.

Possible complications:

- A carriage couples in the wrong order and must be corrected.  
- A crane moves too far and blocks a different track.  
- A switch jams and needs a manual yard run.  
- A loud movement wakes a deeper station subsystem.  
- A damaged carriage can be taken, but starts with a fault.  
- Someone takes a Minor Injury from yard machinery or a bad climb.  
- Supplies from B3 can be consumed to stabilize, patch, wedge, insulate, or power a workaround.

### Block B5.7 — Departure Release

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The Express comes together**  
> Across the yard, the old locomotive shifts with a sound too deep to be only metal. One by one, the couplings close behind it. Lights wake along the train's spine: dim amber in the windows, red pinpoints under the wheels, a pulse from the boiler like something breathing under iron.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The route opens**  
> The final switch turns. The departure rail lines up with a long, black track running out of the station and into the broken world. For the first time since you woke in the chapel, the path ahead is not just visible. It is open.

> [!success] **OUTCOME — Scene complete**  
> - The Offboarder front-door problem is resolved.  
> - The party has accessed the main control room.  
> - The locomotive and starter carriages are aligned / coupled.  
> - The departure route is clear enough to leave the station.  
> - The Apocalypse Express feels awake or nearly awake.  
> - Trigger the next beat: **Lucifer appears again** as an apparition when the party is ready to depart.
