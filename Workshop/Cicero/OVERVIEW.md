# Cicero — Character Architecture

> [!summary]
> Cicero is the recurring companion / chronicler NPC of the Apocalypse Express campaign. He first appears in **A1 Null Meridian** as the unreachable mumbling old man at the obsidian crossroads, and rejoins the player on the train as a Genitivi-style **codex unlocker**. His obsidian-tipped quill is the only material in existence not of Earth; in the **endgame** it is the only weapon capable of wounding Lucifer.

---

## The Lucifer Pact — foundational cosmology

> [!important] The main plot is the **Orb retrieval** (per `Database/40_Scenes/A5 Sundown Saloon`). Cicero is a **parallel side prize** — a second, unconscious commission whose payoff lands at the end as a reveal. Cicero does not drive plot; he colours the climax.

### Two parallel prizes Lucifer collects

The campaign delivers Lucifer **two** things, only one of which the player knows about:

| | The Orb (main plot) | The Manuscript (Cicero's book) |
|---|---|---|
| What | Fist-sized matte black sphere veined with gold; twin sits in a vault at the end of the world | Cicero's leather-bound chronicle, recorded across the journey |
| Player conscious of it? | ✓ explicit deal | ✗ delivered unconsciously through the act of being chronicled |
| Lucifer's use | Resets the timestop / "the pause" → reset instead of ending | Source-document for designing the next universe |
| Delivered when | Climax — players hand it to Lucifer at the end of the world | Climax — Lucifer takes the book alongside the Orb |
| Player's reaction | Heroic conclusion, or betrayal at the brink | Side reveal: "wait, his book too?" |

Cicero is the **mirror** of the Orb. The Orb is hardware; the manuscript is software. Both are commissions Lucifer set up long ago. The player negotiates one and never realises the other was running in parallel.

### Cicero's awareness — the third trick

Cicero **believes** he has read the entire script in his book and is performing the only winning path (Doctor Strange / Endgame parallel — *"out of 14 million futures, this is the one where we win"*). In **reality** he sees only the events, not the meta-meaning. He doesn't know the manuscript is for Lucifer's next universe.

He **doesn't know** he is missing this layer. His confidence is genuine. He performs the role with full sincerity because he believes it is the only path that works.

This is **Lucifer's third commission** — the first two being the Orb (player-conscious) and the manuscript (player-unconscious). The third is **Cicero's confident ignorance**: Lucifer needs Cicero performing without deviation, so the book reveals events but withholds meaning. Cicero never realises his sight is curated.

**The climax double-reveal:** the player learns Lucifer wanted the book all along; Cicero, simultaneously, realises his "complete knowledge" was incomplete. His mastery and his role collapse together. **Two tragedies, one moment.**

### Cicero's role in this

**The deal:**

- Long ago, Cicero wanted to write **the greatest story ever told** — The Story of the World, of Humanity, of the Universe. He wanted to be the best writer who ever existed.
- He made a pact with Lucifer to be granted this opportunity.
- Lucifer found this *useful* and parked Cicero in the Null Meridian — a holding cell, a waiting room — until the right moment.
- That moment is **now**: the players board the train, Cicero boards with them, his commission begins.

**What Cicero thinks he is doing:**

- Writing the definitive chronicle of the world's last journey
- Fulfilling his life's ambition — the masterpiece, the immortal manuscript
- Recording the story of *how Lucifer won over God* — from the winning side, with full access

**What Lucifer is actually doing:**

- Using Cicero's manuscript as a **detailed source-document** for designing the *next* universe
- Wearing an earthly body temporarily to gather material directly (he is *incarnate* during the campaign — this body is disposable)
- Conducting the players to him at the end of the world so the manuscript is delivered in person
- Treating the entire campaign as a research field trip whose deliverable is the book

**The cosmic symmetry:**

| Player thinks they are | Actually they are |
|---|---|
| Heroes saving the world | Unwitting field researchers gathering material |
| Defeating Lucifer | Delivering Lucifer his manuscript |
| Killing him with the quill | Helping him discard his temporary body |

Three actors all believe they are in control. None of them are. Cicero thinks he's the author; he's the *pen*. Lucifer thinks he's the patron; he's the *user*. The party thinks they're the heroes; they're the *subject*.

## Role layers

Cicero operates on **three** narrative tiers simultaneously:

1. **Companion** — recurring NPC the player can talk to across train episodes. Quiet, patient, observant. *Eager once activated* — the long wait is over, the work begins.
2. **System feature** — identify / codex mechanic. Player brings him an object, he writes its truth into his book with the quill, the entry becomes a permanent codex the player can re-read. *(Mechanically: the player thinks they're building their own codex. Narratively: they are populating Lucifer's source document.)*
3. **Cosmic enforcement mechanism** — the act of his quill inscribing the substrate is what makes promises (including Lucifer's contracts) ontologically binding. *Why* Lucifer cannot lie: not as a virtue, as physics — and Cicero is the physics. *(But Cicero is also Lucifer's commissioned chronicler — so the registry-as-truth is itself Lucifer-authored cosmology. The "physics" was set up by the patron who benefits from it.)*

## Mechanics

### The quill

- Made of an unknown material — **the only material in existence not of Earth**
- Obsidian-tipped, gold-shafted; the obsidian glows brighter than the gold (light-physics is *wrong* here — quiet worldbuilding cue)
- The tip is the same material as the obsidian fountain at the Null Meridian crossroads — **geographic origin link** between the quill and the Halt
- Fades / becomes translucent when not in active use, as if it lifts the burden from its master's hand. Not a tool — a partner.
- Writes by **cutting** the page rather than depositing ink; lines are surgical incisions that catch light from beneath. Foreshadow for the endgame use as a weapon.
- Cannot be wielded by anyone other than its bonded carrier (TBD — see [QUESTIONS](QUESTIONS.md))

### The book

- Bound in dark leather; pages cream-coloured, edges trimmed clean. Doesn't belong here. Neither does the quill. But the man holding them does.
- Functions as a **registry of souls** — names, dates, status marks (✓ / ✗)
- Every soul who passes through Cicero's domain is recorded; the act of recording with the quill makes their fate binding in the substrate
- TBD: name of the book, what happens if it is destroyed/stolen, whether the player can ever read it directly (see [QUESTIONS](QUESTIONS.md))

### Identify mechanic (codex)

- Player finds object → brings to Cicero → he writes about it with the quill → the truth of the object is revealed
- Each identify becomes a permanent **codex entry** the player can re-read
- Rate-limits lore drops to where Cicero is present (mostly the train, after he opens up)
- Creates emotional cost for the player at Cicero's potential sacrifice — the lore-curious player has more to lose. The system scales the emotional stake by player engagement.

## Visual / character anchors

- Heavy dark cloak, hooded, hunched but not frail
- Forgettable face — your eye slides off him unless the quill catches the light
- His shadow falls in a way that suggests a different light source than the local one (light bends differently around him)
- Obsidian-tipped golden quill, leather-bound book that doesn't quite belong in this place
- Fits *too* perfectly here — the crossroads grew around him, or he grew out of it

## Foreshadow timeline — quill as endgame weapon

| Where | Anchor | Function |
|---|---|---|
