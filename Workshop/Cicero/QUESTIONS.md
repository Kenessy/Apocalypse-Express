## Naming — DECIDED 2026-04-25

> Is "Cicero" his canonical name, or a placeholder?

**Decision: Cicero (canonical).**

**Rationale.** The name does narrative work on two levels:

- **Surface:** Cicero is *familiar* to most players — they recognise it vaguely from history class as "some philosopher, antiquity". This makes it feel anchored and literary, but not pinned to a specific era or culture. Avuncular, non-threatening. Matches the character's surface (quiet old man writing in a book). The name is *safe-sounding* — and that itself is a deception.
- **Underneath:** the historical Marcus Tullius Cicero was a Roman orator, defender of the Republic, who wrote on duty, justice, and the binding nature of promises — and was beheaded for speaking truth to power. The pen as the sword was already his metaphor. The character's deeper layers (registry-keeper, end-game sacrifice, the only weapon that can hurt Lucifer) map onto this directly.

The "safe name" is itself part of how the character hides in plain sight — exactly how Cicero-NPC wants to be seen.

## Relationship to Lucifer — DECIDED 2026-04-25

> Is Cicero Lucifer's employee, hostage, rival, neutral party, or something else?

**Decision: Commissioned chronicler under a long-standing pact.**

Cicero made a deal with Lucifer to be granted the chance to write **the greatest story ever told**. Lucifer found this useful and parked Cicero in the Null Meridian as a holding cell until the moment was right. The party's journey *is* the moment. Cicero believes he is fulfilling his life's ambition; Lucifer is using his manuscript as a source-document for designing the next universe.

See OVERVIEW → "The Lucifer Pact" section for the full structure.

**Power dynamic implications:**

- Cicero is bound to Lucifer by his own desire, not by force
- Lucifer cannot easily harm Cicero (he needs the manuscript) — but Cicero is also no threat to Lucifer (the deal is in Lucifer's favour)
- The endgame sacrifice carries the bitter irony that Cicero's "great work" is taken by the patron who commissioned it; even his death may not break the cycle

## Sacrifice mechanic — DECIDED 2026-04-25

> What does it cost to use the quill on Lucifer?

**Decision: Combined cost — Cicero dies + quill consumed + book taken by Lucifer.**

The act of striking Lucifer with the quill ends Cicero, destroys the quill (one-shot weapon), and the book is taken by Lucifer down to hell — to serve as the source-document for designing the next universe.

**The bitter twist:** Lucifer does not resist the wounding. He laughs, discards his earthly body, and walks into the fire willingly — *"if you want to kill me, I'll help. I don't need this body anymore."* The "victory" hands him exactly what he came for: the manuscript. The party "wins" but actually delivers the prize.

This raises the stakes vertically rather than horizontally — every loss is real, but the implied victory is hollow. The player has to live with the knowledge that the cycle continues, the next universe is being designed from their own journey, and their heroism was the patron's harvest.

## Does Cicero KNOW Lucifer's plan for the manuscript? — DECIDED 2026-04-25

> Resolved as the **hybrid "thinks-he-knows-everything-but-doesn't"** structure. Doctor Strange / Endgame parallel: he believes he sees the only winning path; in fact he only sees the events, not their meta-meaning. He doesn't know that he doesn't know.

**Decision: Cicero believes E1 (knows all), is actually E2 (only knows events), and is unaware of the gap.**

**How it plays:**

- **Confidence is his trademark throughout.** He smiles before changes happen because he "knows". He is calm because he "sees" the end. This confidence carries the character — and is what he loses at the climax.
- **His performance is clean and warm.** No double-life angst. He genuinely enjoys the players' company; he plays his role openly because he believes it is the only winning path.
- **The "they cannot lie" rule applies in a new way:** he doesn't lie because *he doesn't know he would be lying*. When he says "this is the only good path," he says it with full sincerity — even though he is wrong.
- **The climax double-reveal:** the player learns Lucifer wanted the book all along; Cicero learns *simultaneously* that his book never showed him the truth. **Two tragedies, one moment.** His mastery and his role both collapse together.

**Sub-question — who curated Cicero's blind spot?**

- **(α) Lucifer set it up.** The most dramatic version. Lucifer gave Cicero the book and quill with the constraint built in — events visible, meta-meaning hidden. Lucifer needs Cicero to perform without deviation. **The third trick (after Orb and manuscript): Cicero's confident ignorance was its own commission.** Recommended.
- **(β) Cicero's own vanity.** The book would have shown more if he'd looked harder. He fell in love with what he could see and never read the margins. *"I would have known if I had asked."*
- **(γ) The nature of the book itself.** Books only record events, never meaning. Anyone writing in it would be in the same position. Not Lucifer's trick — a fundamental limit of the medium.

## Is Cicero unique, or one in a series?

> The user previously asked "are there others like him". With the new cosmology, this question gets sharper:

- **(A) Unique.** Lucifer commissioned only Cicero. Maximum stakes — there is no replacement.
- **(B) One in a series of failed predecessors.** Lucifer has commissioned others before; they failed (broke, died early, wrote badly, refused). Cicero is the current bet, and he succeeded where others did not. This adds melancholy ("I am the last") and explains his confidence.
- **(C) The latest of MANY successful predecessors.** Universes have been written and re-designed many times. Each cycle has its Cicero. The cycle is ancient.

(C) implies the entire AE world is a *copy* of an earlier world Lucifer designed from a previous Cicero's manuscript. Massively cosmic; possibly too much.
