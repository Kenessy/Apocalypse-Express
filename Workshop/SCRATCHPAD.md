## 2026-04-25 — Cosmology lock-in (the Lucifer Pact)

**Major architectural decision** — see Cicero/OVERVIEW.md "The Lucifer Pact" section.

The whole campaign re-frames as **Lucifer's research expedition**. Cicero is his commissioned chronicler. The party are the subject. The book is the source-document for designing the next universe. The endgame "victory" is Lucifer's actual prize-collection moment.

**Three layers of self-deception:**
- Cicero thinks he's the author → he's the pen
- Lucifer thinks he's the patron → he's the user
- Players think they're the heroes → they're the subject

**Decisions locked in this conversation:**
- Naming → "Cicero" (canonical)
- Lucifer relationship → commissioned chronicler under long-standing pact
- Opening trigger → train boarding (A3)
- Sacrifice mechanic → Cicero dies + quill consumed + book taken by Lucifer; Lucifer welcomes the wounding because it lets him discard his earthly body

**New questions opened:**
- Does Cicero KNOW Lucifer's plan? (Most important remaining character question — see QUESTIONS.md)
- Is Cicero unique or one in a series of commissioned chroniclers?
- Can the player ever read the book?
- When does the player begin to suspect Cicero?
- Does Cicero have unconscious tells of Lucifer's influence?

**Implications for already-written content:**

The A1 "promise" mumble now has a **specific** meaning — Cicero is waiting for Lucifer to honour their pact. *"How much longer?"* is not rhetorical, it's impatience. Centuries / millennia of waiting. The Null is his **commission's waiting room**. This dramatically charges the A1 scene — the player is meeting a man on the cusp of his life's purpose finally beginning.

The obsidian fountain / quill connection now reads as **Lucifer's gift / loan** rather than neutral substrate. The Null Meridian is *part of Lucifer's apparatus*, not an older neutral place. Cicero's "fits perfectly here" reads as: he was *designed* for this place, or this place was designed *for* him. Either way — Lucifer's furniture.

## 2026-04-25 — Cicero awareness state locked: "thinks he knows, doesn't know he doesn't"

Final structure on Cicero's knowledge:

- He **believes** he has read the entire script in his book and is performing the only winning path (Doctor Strange / Endgame parallel)
- In **reality** he sees only the events, not their meta-meaning. He doesn't know the manuscript is for Lucifer's next universe.
- He **doesn't know** he is missing this layer — his confidence is genuine.
- Recommended cause: **Lucifer set it up** (third prize / third trick). Lucifer needed Cicero performing without deviation, so he kept Cicero's view limited while letting him believe it was complete.

**Climax double-reveal**: the player learns about the manuscript's true purpose at the same moment Cicero realizes his "complete knowledge" was incomplete. His mastery and his role collapse together.

This preserves Cicero's warmth and competence through the entire campaign — no double-life angst — while making the final beat the heaviest possible.

## 2026-04-25 — FUTURE BRANCH: The Fates / fabric-of-fate weavers

Surfaced in conversation. **Not canonical yet** — only loose "fate" mentions in the database, no entities defined. To be built out later.

**Concept seed:** there exist fate-weavers in this cosmology (Norns / Moirai / Hungarian folk-banyák analogue). They weave the tapestry of fate. The fabric can be **torn**. Cicero's book, because it records all events accurately, could potentially be used to **repair tears** in the fabric — making it a multi-purpose artifact.

**This opens new climax branches:**

| Player choice with the book | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Default — let Lucifer take it | Lucifer wins (manuscript = next universe template). Cycle continues. |
| Use it to repair the torn fabric | Subverts Lucifer's plan; possibly prevents apocalypse from happening at all |
| Hybrid — partial repair, partial delivery | Compromise endings |
| Burn it | Refuse to deliver any prize. World ends but no continuity for Lucifer. |
| Cicero realizes and acts himself | Sacrifice path with different consequences |

**Why this is good design space:**

- Gives the players a **meta-choice** at the climax beyond "deliver or betray"
- Introduces a third faction (the Weavers) who could appear earlier in the campaign as foreshadow
- Lucifer might **be the cause** of the tears (apocalypse manipulation), which means the book's repair function is undoing what Lucifer set up
- Creates a "secret good ending" the players can find through investigation
- Cicero's ignorance (he believes only one path works) becomes player-correctable: the players can see what Cicero cannot

2. Mechanic for "tears in the fabric" — how do they manifest in-game
3. Mechanic for using the book to repair — does it cost pages? Cicero's life? Quill?
4. Where in the campaign do tears appear and become noticeable to the players
5. Endings table — write out each branch with outcome description
