Cicero — Loops, free will, the nature of choice. "Wake up."
Morning, noon, evening — twilight by day, darkness by night... Our life is a loop of shadow and light. An endless loop of — well. But what is a loop? A circuit where we wake up, go to work, then fall asleep. Every day, the same exact thing, around and around — like a hamster in a wheel. Neuron A fires to B, B fires to C, C fires back to A, forever repeating the same cycle.

A circuit where current builds forever... or — until it breaches a threshold? In the brain, a synapse... the signal arcs, spikes, cascades — finally shattering its own cycle. So we are in a loop until we reach... what? Death? Whatever the "desired effect" is? We quit? We die? We get promoted? Anything — anything — to break the loop. But here is the thing: the loop won't change. We have to. This is why I was right earlier — in a stable loop, structure emerges. And we organise our entire lives around it.

But what is the purpose of said loop? To keep something — us — in it. As long as necessary. We are the same as our brains: looped through space and time, returning to the same place, processing vast amounts in our connections — giving our time and devotion to those we deem worthy, and ignoring those we don't. The whole thing rests on a single, fragile assumption: that we choose.

But whenever we do... it always feels one-sided, doesn't it? We didn't really have a choice. Did we? Or did the loop decide for us?

Light follows darkness, darkness follows light. What is it that breaks us free? What if we step sideways — out of the loop entirely? Anything that breaks the flow is enough. And every loop ends with the same thing... learning. Becoming more than you were, by any means necessary. Learning... ascending.... ascension... opening your eyes and seeing... understanding... waking up... becoming... something more?

Wake up.

Cicero — Obsidian crossroads, fountain, mist. "Let us choose."
The mist is rising — slowly, deliberately — from the obsidian landscape. Something fully strange, yet awfully familiar. As if you had been here before... in a dream you never quite finished.

Two main roads once crossed here. Travellers stopped to drink from the fountain — an obsidian basin at the heart of the crossroads, its water still crystal-clear. The traces of the living have long since worn away. Footprints eroded, carts dissolved into dust, voices swallowed by the fog. But the water remains. Pure. Patient.

The land did not decay. Only those who walked it. Now it waits — masterless — for a new soul. Someone willing to step into a new adventure. A new loop. Out of the frying pan and into the fire, as they say.

At least this loop is fast. The roads lead to the same place regardless of the traveller. But it is the one who chooses that carries the meaning. A highway is nothing without its wanderers. A crossroads is nothing without the souls who dare to cross to the other side.

So then — let us choose. Nothing simpler than that.

— Someone, somewhere.

Whole groves in places, dark-leaved and strange, clinging to pockets of dirt that have no business being here. Life — temporary, borrowed, growing on borrowed ground.

Two failures have changed the equation. The difficulty isn't in the stone anymore. It's in you. In whether you have anything left that physics can't explain.

The landscape doesn't change. The same obsidian underfoot, the same grey nothing in every direction. You walk for what feels like minutes but could be seconds — distance is meaningless here. The fog eats depth, swallows scale. Everything is always the same distance away: too far to see.

You're about to turn back when you spot it. A shape in the mist — solid, dark, not obsidian. A tree. Thick-trunked, dark-leaved, growing from a crack where soil has gathered over centuries. It's not the obelisk. But it's something.

You coil your legs and jump. Your fingers brush the bark — wet, cold, uncertain. If the branch holds, you're up. If it doesn't...

Fountain Branch — Examine the Fountain

examine-fountain DONE ⊙ look closer
Basin carved from single obsidian piece. Water crystal-clear — sterile. Remnants at the edge.
You walk to the fountain. Up close, the strangeness sharpens. The basin is carved from a single piece of obsidian — no seams, no joints, no mason's marks. The water pours from somewhere inside the obelisk's base, though there's no visible mechanism. It just... flows. As if it always has.

The water is crystal-clear. Not clean the way a mountain stream is clean — clean the way a vacuum is clean. There is nothing in it. No algae, no sediment, no life. The stones beneath it are ancient but unblemished. Not a single patch of moss. Not a single stain.

Around the fountain, the crossroads stretches in four directions. Remnants are scattered at the basin's edge — things people left behind. You kneel down for a closer look.

Coins rest on the bottom of the basin — different sizes, different metals. Copper, silver, something darker. Some round, some square, some shaped like nothing you've seen before. Wishes, thrown into a fountain where no one listens.

The water is impossibly still between the ripples. Not fresh because it's young — fresh because there is nothing here to contaminate it. No soil to cloud it. No insects to drown in it. It is sterile in a way that nature never is.

Around the basin: the remains of travellers. Cloth rotted to grey threads. Wooden crates collapsed into splinters — only the iron fittings remain, rusted into lace. A carved bone die, yellowed and cracked. A metal button. A stone bead, smooth and pale, threaded on nothing.

Small things. Personal things. Left by people who passed through and kept walking. Or people who stopped and never left. Nothing here will tell you which.

You walk to the nearest road and follow it a few paces. Then the next. Then the third, the fourth. They are identical — the same width, the same obsidian surface, the same fog swallowing them whole within fifty metres.

But the signs are different. Each road has them — posts driven into cracks in the glass, made from stone, metal, rotting wood. The inscriptions are in languages you've never seen. Some carved deep, some painted in pigments that have almost faded to nothing. Numbers beside words that might be names. Distances, maybe. Directions to places.

But as you study them, a feeling creeps in. An intuition. These distances are wrong. Not because the signs are lying — because the world has moved. Whatever these markers once pointed to has shifted, drifted, dissolved. The places they name are no longer where they were. They may no longer be at all.

You step back from the signs. Not from fear. From the quiet certainty that you are reading directions to a dead world. Signposts to nowhere.

The entries get shorter toward the bottom. Less formal. More afraid. "Shipments delayed — third time this cycle. Eastern road compromised. Requesting reinforcements."

"Send word to my son. Tell them to stay home. Do not let his mother leave the house after twilight. Lock everything if I am not back by dark."

The message was never sent. The wax was never broken from the other side. No one folded it for travel. No one read it. Until now.

You approach the old man. He stands a few paces from the fountain, slightly hunched, facing no particular direction. A heavy cloak drapes from his shoulders — the fabric is dark, unremarkable, the kind of thing you'd forget the moment you looked away. There is nothing striking about him. Nothing that would make you look twice.

Except the quill. It catches the light — what little there is — and gleams. The tip is obsidian, honed to a needle point, black and glossy as the ground beneath your feet. It moves across the pages of a small, thick book bound in leather so dark it could be skin. He writes without looking up. Without pausing. As if the words arrive faster than his hand can follow.

The book itself is expensive — that much is clear. The binding is tight, the pages cream-coloured, the edges trimmed clean. It doesn't belong in this place. Neither does the quill. But the man holding them? He fits. He fits perfectly — as if the crossroads grew around him, or he grew out of it.

He hasn't acknowledged you. But he knows you're there. You're certain of it.
