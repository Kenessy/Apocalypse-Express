▶ wake up
Cicero Morning, noon, evening — twilight by day, darkness by night... Our life is a loop of shadow and light. An endless loop of — well. But what is a loop? A circuit where we wake up, go to work, then fall asleep. Every day, the same exact thing, around and around — like a hamster in a wheel.

A circuit where current builds forever... or — until it breaches a threshold? So we are in a loop until we reach... what? Death? Whatever the "desired effect" is? The loop won't change. We have to.

But what is the purpose of said loop? To keep something — us — in it. The whole thing rests on a single, fragile assumption: that we choose.

But whenever we do... it always feels one-sided, doesn't it? Or did the loop decide for us?

Light follows darkness, darkness follows light. What if we step sideways — out of the loop entirely? Every loop ends with the same thing... learning. Becoming more than you were. Waking up...

Wake up.

The land did not decay. Only those who walked it. Now it waits — masterless — for a new soul. Out of the frying pan and into the fire, as they say.

So then — let us choose. Nothing simpler than that.

— Someone, somewhere.

You pull yourself over the final edge. The wind stops. The mist falls away beneath you like a veil being lifted — and for the first time, you see.

An endless obsidian plain. In every direction — the same. The same dark glass, the same ancient fractures, the same silence. Wherever you look, wherever you turn, the answer is identical. There is no edge. No boundary. No end.

Here and there, soil has gathered in the cracks — blown in from nowhere, accumulated over aeons. Patches of earth where stubborn things grow. Bushes. Trees. Whole groves in places, dark-leaved and strange, clinging to pockets of dirt that have no business being here. Life — temporary, borrowed, growing on borrowed ground.

This is an ancient plane. Older than anything you have words for. It operates on rules you do not recognise, built by something you cannot name. It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is simply alien. And above all else — it is old.

You have seen enough. You climb back down — slowly, carefully — and return to the crossroads. The view stays with you. It will not leave easily.
